## The short version

Tesla China did **not** rewrite the Model 3 / Model Y guide-price board in early September. What it announced around **7 September 2026** was a **time-boxed inventory incentive**: cash on eligible stock cars if you order and take delivery by **30 September**.

That distinction matters. Headlines say “cuts.” The mechanism is closer to a quarter-end clearance lever — guide MSRP stays, effective transaction price moves for shoppers who accept inventory configuration and the deadline.

FX used below for approximate USD: **CNY 6.78 / USD** (illustrative, aligned with this edition’s footnotes). Roundings are editorial; do not treat them as official Tesla FX.

## Incentive mechanics (full public picture)

Press-consistent package, dual-sourced across major English and Chinese auto business outlets:

| Element | Public figure | Notes |
|---------|---------------|-------|
| Model 3 inventory cash | about **5,000 RMB** (≈ **$737**) | Eligible stock / inventory configs |
| Model Y / Model Y L inventory cash | about **10,000 RMB** (≈ **$1,475**) | Same window; includes Model Y L in press |
| Window | Order **and** delivery by **30 Sep 2026** | Deadline is the product |
| Guide MSRP | **Reported unchanged** | Hierarchy and residual narrative intact |
| Eligibility | Inventory / stock cars | Config and city variance — verify in-store |

**What this is:** a delivery-dated cash lever on metal already in the system.

**What this is not:** a permanent national guide rewrite, a new powertrain SKU, or a blanket “everyone gets −X” coupon regardless of configuration.

Operational reading for product, BD, and supplier audiences:

1. **Throughput first.** Soft early-September retail backdrop in CPCA-linked press makes quarter-end metal movement the priority.
2. **Sticker politics.** Leaving guides untouched preserves trim hierarchy and residual-value talking points even while transaction prices soften.
3. **Configuration tax.** Shoppers who need a non-inventory color/wheel/option package may not see the same effective math — the incentive is inventory-shaped on purpose.
4. **Financing choreography.** Outlets describing “stacked floors” are describing cash **plus** financing or selected credits under stated conditions — not a second MSRP.

## Stacked-floor interpretation

Some outlets cite stacked floors near **~222,500 RMB** (≈ **$32,817**) for Model 3 and **~253,500 RMB** (≈ **$37,389**) for Model Y under cash **plus** financing / selected credits.

How to read those numbers without confusing them with guide MSRP:

| Layer | What it is | What it is not |
|-------|------------|----------------|
| Guide MSRP | Brochure / national hierarchy | The price every buyer pays |
| Inventory cash | Time-boxed, config-gated cash | A permanent cut |
| Stacked floor | Effective under **stated** cash + financing/credit stacks | A new official MSRP |
| Street / negotiated | Dealer- and city-specific | National policy |

**Analytical rule:** if the stacked floor is ~13k below guide on Model 3 (235.5k → ~222.5k) while inventory cash alone is ~5k, the residual ~8k is coming from **non-MSRP** levers (financing, credits, or other benefits as reported). Treat the whole stack as **conditional entitlement math**, not a sticker rewrite.

For competitive comparisons, prefer:

- **Guide vs guide** when discussing brand positioning and residual narratives  
- **Effective vs effective** when discussing what a September shopper actually sees  
- Explicit labels when mixing the two — mixing them is how headlines invent “cuts” that never happened on the board

## Competitive so-what vs Xiaomi / BYD / BMW

September’s China EV shelf did not wait for Tesla to rewrite guides. Public figures already in this pack:

| Peer move | Public figure | Overlap with Tesla shoppers |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------------------|
| Xiaomi **Sky Nomad** EREV SUV family (7 Sep) | Guides from **209,900 RMB** (≈ **$30,959**); N70/N90 band up through **299,900 RMB** | Steals consideration from Model Y shoppers who were never pure-BEV ideologues |
| BYD **Qin Max / Seal 06** family | Entry shelf ~**99,900 RMB** (≈ **$14,735**), mid-August launches still shaping September inventory | Compresses the whole ladder below Tesla; keeps “smart sedan for ~100k” as the gravitational floor |
| BMW **iX3 LWB** pre-sale | From **269,900 RMB** (≈ **$39,808**), deliveries toward Nov in press | Premium JV product sitting near Model Y money with a different brand promise |

**Net competitive read:**

- Tesla’s September move is rational **tactical** pricing — volume defense via inventory + deadline — **not** a strategic admission that the guide architecture collapsed.
- Xiaomi’s Sky Nomad launch puts **EREV family SUV** guides into the same calendar week as Tesla’s inventory sprint. The consideration set for large-SUV shoppers now includes software-brand EREV math next to Tesla BEV guides.
- BYD’s ~100k dual-network sedan shelf does not “compete trim-for-trim” with Model 3/Y; it **anchors the floor** so every brand above it must justify the step-up with clearer effective-offer math.
- BMW’s iX3 LWB pre-sale shows premium JV willingness to sit in the Model Y conversation band — brand promise and delivery timing differ, but the RMB conversation overlaps.

If you only track “did Tesla cut the sticker?”, you will mis-read September. The competitive tell is **inventory + deadline + financing choreography** — the same grammar domestic rivals have been using in “Golden September” entitlement packages.

## Why this is volume defense, not a new product story

China’s passenger-car retail backdrop in early September reporting still looked soft on a YoY basis (CPCA-linked press). In that climate, Tesla’s core sedan/SUV pair is defending **throughput** against:

- Local BEVs that undercut on hardware features per RMB  
- EREVs that sell “range anxiety solved” without asking buyers to live on public charging  
- Entitlement stacks that make transaction math opaque on purpose  

An inventory sprint lets Tesla move metal **without** resetting the national guide board that anchors residual-value narratives and trim hierarchy. There was **no new China powertrain SKU** in the first 10 days of September in the public record Apex Lane used — incentive depth is the story.
